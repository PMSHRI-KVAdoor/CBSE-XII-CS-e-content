1. Introduction to Computer Networks

Basic Computer Network

Data travels from one device to another through network devices and communication media.

A computer network is a collection of two or more interconnected computing devices that can communicate with one another and share data and resources.

Why do we need computer networks?

  • Resource sharing: Printers, storage devices, applications and Internet connections can be shared.
  • Data sharing: Users can exchange files and information.
  • Communication: Email, messaging, video conferencing and other services are possible.
  • Centralised services: Servers can provide common services to many clients.
  • Reliability: Data and services can sometimes be made available through alternative devices or paths.

Basic idea

When one computer sends information to another, the information travels through a communication medium according to agreed rules called protocols.

Example: When a student accesses a file stored on a school server, the student's computer acts as a sender/receiver depending on the direction of communication, the server participates in the communication, the file is the message/data, the network medium carries the data, and protocols control the exchange.

The modern Internet developed gradually from earlier research and academic networks. The syllabus specifically highlights ARPANET, NSFNET and the Internet.

ARPANET

ARPANET stands for Advanced Research Projects Agency Network. It was developed under the U.S. Department of Defense's Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) and became one of the earliest important packet-switching computer networks.

ARPANET demonstrated that computers at geographically separated locations could communicate over a network. Packet switching was an important concept associated with its development.

NSFNET

NSFNET stands for National Science Foundation Network. It was established by the U.S. National Science Foundation to support communication among academic and research institutions.

NSFNET became an important backbone for the growing network of networks and helped expand Internet connectivity beyond the original research environment.

Internet

The Internet is a worldwide system of interconnected computer networks. It allows devices and networks around the world to communicate using common protocols, particularly the TCP/IP protocol suite.

Services such as the World Wide Web, email, file transfer and online communication operate over the Internet.

Easy timeline to remember

ARPANET → NSFNET → Growth and interconnection of networks → Internet

Protocol provides the rules followed during the whole exchange.

Data communication means the exchange of data between two or more devices through some form of communication medium.

Characteristics of effective communication

  • The sender and receiver should be correctly identified.
  • The data should reach the intended destination.
  • The communication should follow agreed protocols.
  • The medium should be suitable for the required communication.

4. Communication Media

A communication medium is the physical or wireless path used to carry data from sender to receiver.

Broad categories

Guided / Wired Media
Signals travel through a physical path such as twisted-pair cable, coaxial cable or optical fibre.
Unguided / Wireless Media
Signals travel through the air or space using electromagnetic waves, such as radio or microwave communication.

Exam point: The choice of medium depends on factors such as required speed, distance, cost, reliability and environment.

5. Protocols

A protocol is a set of rules that controls how data is formatted, transmitted and received between communicating devices.

Why are protocols needed?

  • They provide a common set of communication rules.
  • They help devices understand the structure and meaning of exchanged data.
  • They support reliable and organised communication.
Simple analogy: Two people need a common language and rules of conversation to communicate effectively. Similarly, network devices need protocols.

Data transfer rate tells how much data is transferred per unit time.

Bandwidth

Bandwidth describes the capacity of a communication channel. In networking contexts, higher bandwidth generally means that more data can be transmitted per unit time.

Bandwidth may be expressed in units such as bits per second (bps) when referring to digital transmission capacity, or in Hz for the frequency range of an analogue communication channel. Always read the context of the question.

Data Transfer Rate

Data transfer rate is the amount of data transferred from one point to another per unit time. It is commonly measured in bps, Kbps, Mbps or Gbps.

UnitMeaning
1 Kbps10³ bits per second (using decimal networking convention)
1 Mbps10⁶ bits per second
1 Gbps10⁹ bits per second

Important numerical relationship

1 Byte = 8 bits.

Therefore, if a connection transfers data at 80 Mbps, its theoretical rate in MB/s is:

80 ÷ 8 = 10 MB/s

Actual file-transfer speed can be lower because of protocol overhead, congestion and other factors.

Four octets × 8 bits = 32 bits.

An IP address (Internet Protocol address) is a logical address used to identify a device or network interface for communication on an IP network.

IPv4

IPv4 uses a 32-bit address. It is normally written in dotted-decimal notation as four decimal numbers separated by dots.

Example: 192.168.1.10

Each of the four parts represents 8 bits, so:

8 + 8 + 8 + 8 = 32 bits

Why is an IP address important?

It helps identify the source and destination of IP packets so that data can be delivered between devices/networks.

Switching is the technique used to move data from a source to a destination through a communication network.

A. Circuit Switching

In circuit switching, a dedicated communication path is established between the communicating parties before data transfer begins. Network resources are reserved for that connection for its duration.

Advantages: predictable/dedicated path and consistent connection once established.

Limitation: reserved capacity can remain unused during periods when no data is being sent.

Traditional telephone communication is a classic example.

B. Packet Switching

In packet switching, a message is divided into smaller units called packets. Packets are transmitted over a shared network and are handled by network devices as they travel toward the destination.

Advantages: efficient use of shared network resources and well suited to bursty computer communication.

Example: Internet data communication.

9. Circuit Switching vs Packet Switching – Quick Concept Check

Think Circuit: First establish a dedicated road, then use it for the communication.
Think Packet: Break the message into packets and send them through a shared network.

Competency hint: If a question describes continuous dedicated resource reservation, think circuit switching. If it describes data divided into packets and shared network resources, think packet switching.

10. CBSE Exam-Oriented Key Points

  • ARPANET is an important ancestor of today's Internet and is associated with early packet-switching networking.
  • NSFNET helped connect academic and research institutions and supported Internet growth.
  • The Internet is a global network of interconnected networks.
  • The five data communication components are sender, receiver, message, communication medium and protocol.
  • Bandwidth represents communication-channel capacity; data transfer rate expresses how much data is transferred per unit time.
  • IPv4 uses 32 bits and dotted-decimal notation.
  • Circuit switching establishes a dedicated path.
  • Packet switching divides data into packets and uses shared network resources.
  • 1 Byte = 8 bits.
