2. Twisted Pair Cable

Twisted Pair Cable: sheath, insulated conductor pairs and repeated twisting

Twisted pair consists of pairs of insulated copper wires twisted together. The twisting helps reduce interference.

UTP
Unshielded Twisted Pair — no additional metallic shielding.
STP
Shielded Twisted Pair — includes shielding to reduce interference.

Applications: computer networks and telephone/network connections.

Advantages: relatively low cost and easy installation. Limitations: more susceptible to interference than fiber-optic media.

3. Co-axial Cable

Co-axial cable cross-section: Copper Conductor → Insulator → Braided Metal Conductor → Protective Plastic Layer

Copper conductor
Carries the signal.
Insulator
Separates the conductor from the metallic shield.
Braided metal conductor
Provides shielding and helps reduce interference.
Protective plastic layer
Provides physical protection.

Co-axial cable is used for cable communication and provides shielding around the central conductor.

4. Fiber-optic Cable

Fiber-optic cable cross-sectional visual with fiber core, cladding, protective plastic coating and the path of the light ray.

Fiber-optic cable carries data using light signals. It supports high speed, high bandwidth and long-distance transmission and is resistant to electromagnetic interference.

⚡ High speed
📊 High bandwidth
🌐 Long distance
🛡️ Resistant to electromagnetic interference

When is fiber preferred?

When very high-speed, high-bandwidth and long-distance communication with minimum electromagnetic interference is required.
